Ultimate Mountain Bike Guide

Everything you need to choose, size, and maintain your MTB: suspension, geometry, wheel size, tyres, brakes, drivetrains, and more.

MTB Disciplines

XC (Cross-Country)

Lightweight bikes optimised for efficiency and climbing. Typical travel: 100–120mm. Steeper head tube angles (67–69°), fast-rolling tyres, focus on pedalling performance.

Trail

The all‑rounder. 120–150mm travel, balanced geometry (64.5–66.5° HTA), grippy tyres, dropper posts, and robust brakes for mixed terrain.

Enduro

Built for timed descents with big stages. 150–170mm travel, slack front ends (63.5–65.5°), longer wheelbases for stability, tough components.

Downhill

200mm travel, dual‑crown forks, ultra‑slack HTA (62–63.5°), heavy‑duty frames and brakes for steep, technical race tracks.

Dirt Jump

Strong hardtails, single speed or short drivetrains, compact geometry for jumps, skatepark, and pumptracks.

e‑MTB

Motor‑assisted climbing with robust drivetrains, reinforced frames and wheels; travel usually 140–170mm.

Suspension: Hardtail vs Full‑Suspension

Hardtail

  • Lighter and lower maintenance
  • Better power transfer on smooth climbs
  • Great for XC, pumptracks, and skills development

Full‑Suspension

  • Front + rear shock absorbs rough terrain
  • More traction and control on descents
  • Ideal for trail, enduro, and downhill

Tip: Pick suspension travel to match your terrain. More travel increases comfort and confidence on rough descents but adds weight.

Geometry — The Big Four

Head Tube Angle (HTA)
Slack (62–66°) = stability at speed; steep (67–69°) = agile climbing.
Seat Tube Angle (STA)
Steeper (75–78°) keeps you centred for efficient climbing.
Wheelbase
Longer = stable and planted; shorter = nimble in tight turns.
Reach
Longer reach improves stability and modern cockpit fit; size accordingly.

Wheel Sizes & Tyres

  • 29” — Rolls over obstacles, great high‑speed stability; popular in XC/Trail/Enduro.
  • 27.5” — More playful and agile; common in DJ and some enduro builds.
  • Mullet — 29” front / 27.5” rear for rollover + agility; popular in enduro/DH.

Tyre choice: Softer compounds (max grip) vs harder (durability/rolling). Tread patterns: aggressive for wet/loose, faster centre tread for dry/hardpack. Widths 2.3–2.6” are common.

Components to Consider

Drivetrain

1x setups dominate. 10–12 speed cassettes. Clutch derailleurs for chain control (Shimano/SRAM).

Brakes

Hydraulic discs (2–4 pistons). Rotor size 180–220mm based on rider weight and discipline.

Suspension

Air shocks for adjustability; coil for sensitivity and consistency (often DH/Enduro).

Dropper Post

Essential for trail/enduro. Travel 125–200mm+ depending on frame and rider height.

Wheelsets

Alloy for value and toughness; carbon for stiffness/weight. Choose rim width to suit tyre (e.g., 30mm internal).

Cockpit

Bar width (760–800mm) and stem length (35–50mm) impact handling; wider bars add control.

Maintenance Schedule

After Every Ride

  • Wipe drivetrain; relube chain if wet/muddy
  • Check tyre pressure and quick‑releases/axles
  • Inspect pads/rotors for contamination

Regular

  • Suspension lower leg / air can service (30–50hrs)
  • Full bleed for brakes (6–12 months)
  • Linkage bearings (inspect seasonally)

Sizing Guide

Check manufacturer charts, but consider both height and reach preference. If between sizes, pick based on riding style: smaller for playful, larger for stability.
